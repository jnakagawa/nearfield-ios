Technique: band-moiré magnification / sampling-moiré (Hutley; Kamal, Voelkel & Alda 1998; Amidror; Hersch & Chosson band-moiré, SIGGRAPH 2004 — the effect on banknote threads). 1-D line (default, best for text): base = thin bands at pitch pb, each a vertically-squashed whole wordmark; cling = a line grating at pr near pb; sampling magnifies vertically by M = pr/(pr-pb) while horizontal stays 1:1, so letters keep full crispness. A cling shift moves the reveal by M times the shift (optical speedup) — translation just repositions the (periodic) word, so it's essentially free. Wrong pr just rescales M — forgiving to about 10-15%. The catch: a rotation tilts the reveal by M times the angle, which for a 1-D magnifier shears the vertical mapping, so it holds only to about 0.5-1°. 2-D dot mode tiles the same idea into a few LARGE copies: the base lays the whole wordmark across a wide period and squashes it into thin bands; a lattice of apertures that are wide in x (full letter detail) and short in y magnifies vertically by My = pc/(pc-pb), so a handful of big, bold copies fill the field (lower the pitch to grow them). The wide-x aperture keeps text bold and legible but, like 1-D, shears under rotation (~0.6-1°). Drag the canvas to move the cling; arrow keys nudge (Shift = 5px).
